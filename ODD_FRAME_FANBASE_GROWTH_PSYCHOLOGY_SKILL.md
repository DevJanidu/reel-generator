## 1. The fundamental difference: reach vs fandom

A creator must optimize two separate questions:

- **Per-Reel discovery:** Why would an unfamiliar viewer stop, understand the story, keep watching and perhaps share it?
- **Page loyalty:** Why would the *same* person recognize ODD FRAME tomorrow, remember that the last experience was worthwhile, follow it, and voluntarily seek the next story?

**Working growth loop (strategic model, NOT a published Meta algorithm):**

`Distinct opening image → fast comprehension → specific curiosity → character stakes → escalating developments → earned payoff → emotional memory → brand recognition → optional follow/share → another satisfying story → repeat viewing`

A Reel can have huge views and poor fan conversion. A smaller Reel can create stronger future demand. Track **discoverability, satisfaction and follower conversion separately**.

### 1.1 Six distinct decisions a viewer makes

| Viewer decision | Internal question | Psychological mechanism | Creative response | Observable metric / signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stop | “Is this worth a second?” | Salience + immediate meaningful contrast | Start on an understandable problem, revelation or unusual event | Early retention/drop-off *if shown* |
| Understand | “What is happening?” | Narrative comprehension / low unnecessary cognitive load | One protagonist/object, readable relationship and stakes | Steep early drop-off; qualitative viewer confusion |
| Stay | “What will happen?” | Information-gap curiosity | A **specific** unresolved question plus progressive clues | Average watch time; retention by timestamp |
| Care | “Why does this matter?” | Identification + empathy | A want, fear, choice, loss or meaningful risk | Completion, character-focused comments |
| Share | “Who would enjoy this?” | Emotion + interpersonal relevance | Earned awe, tension, satisfaction, humor or touching moment | Shares per 1,000 reached |
| Return/follow | “Will this creator deliver again?” | Familiarity, consistent expectation, trust, community | Consistent art/story quality, memorable promise, useful page identity | Follows per 1,000 non-follower reach; return signals if available |

**Important:** A person can make these decisions without being conscious of them. They are design questions, not exact neuroscientific steps or claims about dopamine.

## 2. Evidence-based psychological mechanisms translated into craft

### P1 — Curiosity needs *known context* plus *missing information*

**Evidence:** Loewenstein's information-gap account describes curiosity as arising from awareness of a gap in knowledge. **Creative inference:** Make the missing piece precisely understandable. Pure ambiguity causes confusion, not reliably sustained attention.

- Weak: “Something unbelievable happened in this forest.” (No concrete gap.)
- Better: “The fox locked the door from the inside. At sunrise, the key was hanging outside.” (We know enough to wonder **how**.)
- Poor retention tactic: withhold everything until the last second.
- Strong tactic: reveal one answer, then let that answer change the stakes.

**Test:** Show the opening still and first sentence to someone unfamiliar; ask, “What happened?” and “What do you want answered?” If both are unclear, rewrite.

### P2 — Identification starts with a goal, not an elaborate face

**Evidence:** Research on identification with narrative characters includes perspective-taking, understanding and emotional involvement. **Creative inference:** An expressive 2D rabbit with one readable desire can engage more effectively than a highly detailed but narratively empty figure.

- Introduce **want**: The rabbit wants the train to stop.
- Show **obstacle**: It never stops at that station.
- Reveal **cost**: Someone on board is waiting for a signal.
- Show **choice**: She must decide whether to leave the lantern burning.

Give a character **specific behavior, vulnerability and agency**. Reject characters who only stand there being described as “sad” or “evil.”

### P3 — Narrative transportation depends on attention, understanding and feeling

**Evidence:** Narrative transportation literature describes mentally entering and becoming absorbed in a story. Its effectiveness is not reducible to fast cuts. **Creative inference:** Every cut should help the audience comprehend what changed.

- Use one shot for location and relationship, then a clue/detail, a reaction, and the consequence.
- Keep object counts, direction, clothing and times consistent so viewers do not need to reconstruct the story.
- Use short lines, natural pauses, sound design and visual causality.
- A static hold can be powerful **when a viewer needs to recognize a revelation**.

### P4 — Emotional activation can encourage sharing; sadness alone is not a universal virality trick

**Evidence:** Berger & Milkman studied online sharing and found that arousal is important; a separate study of viral videos also points to high-arousal emotions. **Do not treat these studies as Facebook 2026 algorithm weights or causal guarantees for AI 2D Reels.**

Potential **earned** share moments:
- Awe: The tiny animal performs an unexpectedly brave act.
- Tension: We understand the cost of a choice before the protagonist does.
- Satisfaction: A clever reversal resolves a meaningful conflict.
- Warmth: A reunion follows believable sacrifice.
- Dark humor: A surprising but coherent contradiction lands.

**Important:** Not every Reel needs an intense emotion. Quiet sadness, eerie ambiguity or delicate romance may build brand affection, even when they are not the best for broad sharing.

### P5 — Memory is helped by consistent *signals*, not repetitive plots

**Creative hypothesis:** Repeatedly recognizable dark illustrated backgrounds, one golden story clue, a consistent narrator, strong sound design and an ODD FRAME page identity may help viewers attribute memories to the brand. Treat as a testable design strategy, not a claim that a particular hex color causes retention.

**System:** Repeat **the presentation language**, not the exact fox/betrayal/karma formula. A fan should recognize “That feels like ODD FRAME” without knowing the plot in advance.

### P6 — Social affinity develops from repeated experiences, not follower begging

**Evidence:** Parasocial media research documents connections people can form with media figures and characters, but much of it focuses on real creators/influencers. **Creative extrapolation:** For ODD FRAME, brand voice, optional story universes, memorable character moments and respectful replies could create affinity.

Good fan-building behaviors:
- Deliver complete stories more often than teaser fragments.
- Ask honest, specific discussion questions *occasionally* (e.g., “At what moment did you realize the bird knew?”), only if they fit the story.
- Reply to substantive comments with real attention; observe requests for genres and characters.
- Use sequel stories only when the continuation genuinely earns its place.
- Let fans have opinions about characters without tricking them into artificial engagement.

## 3. The 0–5-second opening protocol

**Goal:** A strong still and the first spoken sentence must make the core scene understandable and create a *specific* question.

### The three-part visual/voice recipe

1. **0.0 s — Show the event, not the logo.** A visible contradiction, danger, loss, startling exchange, odd object or meaningful imbalance.
2. **0–2.5 s — Say what changed.** Natural sentence; no “Once upon a time,” vague rhetorical question or long backstory.
3. **By 5 s — State or imply the question.** Make the viewer curious about a choice, discovery or consequence.

| Hook type | Example first line | First-still direction | Viewer question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impossible clue | “The key was outside. The door was locked from within.” | Matte gold key outside dark door | Who moved the key? |
| Missing companion | “The wolf came back with three shadows. He'd left with four.” | Wolf and three readable silhouettes | Who is missing? |
| Emotional reversal | “She gave away the only ticket that could take her home.” | Rabbit passing golden ticket to stranger | Why would she do that? |
| Threat with stakes | “Every lantern went out—except the one beneath the lake.” | Dark shoreline; one real gold light source underwater | What is below? |
| Clever situation | “The king offered a reward for finding a thief who had stolen nothing.” | Empty display case, king, meaningful gold signet ring | What was really taken? |

These are **original illustrative starters**, not fully verified story pitches. Expand only if the middle and ending can honor the implied promise.

**Opening gate (must pass all):**
- [ ] Image reads at phone size and in under ~1 second at a glance (creative target, not quantified finding).
- [ ] The first narration line is concrete and understandable.
- [ ] The audience can name a specific unanswered question.
- [ ] At least one relationship, object or consequence matters.
- [ ] One purposeful focal attention zone, gold used logically.
- [ ] No generic brand sting, title screen or exposition delay.

## 4. Retention architecture: promise, progression, payoff

**Default 45–60-second experiment** (adapt to real voiceover; do not force timing):

| Phase | Starting guide | Viewer need | What must change | Useful shot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–5 s | What happened? | Introduce concrete tension/question | Medium/detail contradiction |
| Orientation | 5–12 s | Why does it matter? | Reveal character goal and stakes | Character + environment |
| Complication | 12–25 s | What makes it difficult? | First obstacle or new clue | Reaction; consequence |
| Reframe | 25–40 s | What did I misunderstand? | Partial answer or higher-stakes decision | Significant detail / contrast |
| Resolution | 40–60 s | Was it worth staying? | Pay off the promised outcome | Clear action/consequence; breathing room |

**Micro-payoff rule:** At several points, answer a small question or materially advance the conflict. “More mystery” without new information is not progression.

**Change-of-still rule:** Switch only when the viewer needs to see something new: an action, clue, reaction, scene transition, choice, changed relationship, consequence or reveal. The 2–4-second hold guideline is a useful starting range, not a cut-every-3-seconds mandate.

**Do not:** use artificial motion, endless suspense loops, repeating the same close-up, a new random object every two seconds, loud sound spikes just to startle, misleading endings, or unrelated cliffhangers.

### Diagnostic: the mid-story sag

If viewers leave around the middle, investigate **which promise stopped progressing** before attempting faster zooms:
1. Did the story repeat facts rather than change them?
2. Did the protagonist lose a clear goal?
3. Was a crucial scene missing?
4. Did a sudden setting/character change create confusion?
5. Did the ending become predictable too early?
6. Did narration say everything the image already showed?

## 5. Fan conversion: a page people can choose to follow

**Follower psychology hypothesis:** People follow when they expect another desirable experience from the same source. The Page must make that future value understandable.

### Recognizable identity, without a recurring protagonist

- **Page name:** ODD FRAME.
- **Promise:** *Small Stories. Big Emotions.*
- **Bio proposition:** “Original cinematic 2D short stories. Mystery, heartbreak, strange twists, and everything in between.”
- **Pinned introduction:** A strong *finished story* or 15–25-second original montage of the page's distinct story moods, clearly demonstrating craft; no empty corporate-style intro.
- **Visual signature:** Midnight Gold, recognizable character silhouette treatment, consistent illustration, restrained motion, predictable audio craftsmanship.
- **Editorial signature:** Each story makes a clear promise and honors it. Different genres; consistently satisfying experience.
- **Optional end card:** If used, brief, understated and **after** the payoff. Never interrupt the strongest ending.
- **Follow prompt:** Use sparingly after a strong completed story, e.g., “For more strange little stories, follow ODD FRAME.” Do not attach the same line to every emotional climax.

### Why a viewer would share rather than just like

Before publishing, write two private one-sentence answers:

1. **“A viewer might send this to a friend because ______.”** (e.g., a clever reveal, an uncanny surprise, a heartfelt connection)
2. **“A viewer might follow ODD FRAME because the next Reel is likely to ______.”** (e.g., deliver another short, well-crafted twist or meaningful emotional journey)

If the second answer depends entirely on the same character returning, confirm that a *series* is genuinely intended. Otherwise, strengthen the **brand-level promise**.

### Community practice

- Reply thoughtfully to comments with specific character/story references, not repetitive emoji-only responses.
- Use friendly prompts with genuine narrative relevance; avoid “COMMENT YES,” forced polls and bait questions.
- Keep a **fan vocabulary log**: phrases viewers spontaneously use (“I knew it was the sister,” “This one hurt,” “More mysteries, please”).
- Occasionally showcase a well-received character in another original story **only if** it enhances the creative premise.
- Do not buy followers, join engagement exchanges, use fake comments, or mislead people about story truth.

## 6. Topic/genre portfolio: variety that still feels coherent

The Content Bible allows many genres. Rotate **emotional experiences** rather than uploading six versions of the same betrayal story.

**Initial creative exploration mix (provisional, not an audience fact or rigid quota):**

| Lane | Job for the viewer | Sample premise ingredient |
|---|---|---|
| Mystery & suspense | Wonder / puzzle | An impossible clue with a logical reveal |
| Emotional fiction | Care / warmth / loss | A character makes a costly choice |
| Clever reversal | Satisfaction / surprise | A smart solution changes the power balance |
| Fantasy & adventure | Awe / discovery | Strange worlds with one clear rule |
| Uneasy / dark irony | Fascination / chilling aftertaste | A final image reframes the story |
| Humor / lightness | Relief / delight | A clever comic misunderstanding |

**Testing rule:** Aim for enough variety to discover genuine audience preferences while keeping a core visual voice. Do not overfit the last viral Reel. A breakout mystery does not prove that comedy or warmth must be permanently abandoned.

### “Oddness” is a curiosity hook, not a license for nonsense

ODD FRAME should be **unusual but intelligible**. Show the rules of a fantasy world before subverting them. Avoid random reveals that cannot be understood in hindsight.

## 7. Still-image visual psychology and editorial craft

The gold accent is a *visual priority system*, not a proven “yellow = viral” effect.

**Each generated still prompt must answer:**

> The viewer should notice **[one specific thing]** first because **[the story consequence]**.

### Scene design instructions

1. Use one stable style: hand-drawn cinematic 2D, dark ink contour, flat local color and limited stepped shading. All species, objects and backgrounds share the same graphic language.
2. Prefer readable silhouettes, clear poses, meaningful distances and sparse foreground interruptions. Avoid clutter and AI-prone hand/body interactions.
3. Reserve `#FFD43B` for a **single meaningful focal region** when gold is useful. A matte gold object should not radiate magical light. Use physical illumination only where justified.
4. Frame for vertical 9:16. Start with Bible's conservative essential-content zone approximately x=90–890, y=200–1490 on a 1080×1920 canvas; verify the actual Facebook interface because overlays vary.
5. Compose for understanding: location → object clue → character response → consequence. Vary wide/medium/detail/reaction shots; no redundant holds.
6. Do not ask Google Flow to draw subtitles, watermarks or accidental textual overlays in images. Any optional captions are added correctly during editing.
7. Use **stills only**; cut, hold, selectively reframe, optionally use subtle editor-based parallax and sound design in Remotion. Never interpret “2D animated storytelling” as permission to add generated video jobs.
8. Design simple visual proof: if a coin moves, show it in the old location, then the character noticing it missing, then the new location. Never reverse quantities or positions without cause.
9. Test the opening and crucial reveal at small mobile size, with and without sound.

**Editing warning:** Lots of camera movement does not automatically improve retention. A still that carries a revelation may deserve stillness.

## 9. Analytics: measure the full funnel, not just raw views

### Log per Reel (no fabricated values)

`reel_id, post_date_local, post_date_US_target, concept, genre, emotion, character_type, hook_type, opening_line, focal_object, ending_type, duration_seconds, image_count, caption_style, post_time, reach, views, average_watch_time_seconds, retention_curve_available, shares, comments, reactions, new_follows_attributed, follower_reach, nonfollower_reach, top_countries, account_status, production_hours`

Every value can be `N/A` if not provided. A view is **not** necessarily a unique viewer; don't substitute views for reach when normalizing reach-based metrics.

### Calculations to use only when denominators exist

- **Share rate per 1,000 reached:** `1000 × shares / reach`.
- **Follower conversion per 1,000 non-follower reach:** `1000 × new_follows_attributed / nonfollower_reach`. This is an **approximate attribution indicator**, not a platform-standard causal conversion metric. Use only if the time windows and definitions align.
- **Average watch ratio:** `average_watch_time_seconds / duration_seconds`. **Descriptive only**: loops/replays can make it exceed 1 and it is not a completion rate.
- **Non-follower discovery share:** `nonfollower_reach / total_reach` (if mutually consistent dimensions are available).
- **Engagement by reach:** `(shares + comments + reactions) / reach`, if definitions and measurement windows are comparable. Do not treat as a Meta algorithm score.
- **US share of reach:** `US_reach / geolocated_total_reach`, when Meta provides compatible fields; unknown geographies and views/reach differences must not be silently dropped.
- **Production efficiency:** creator hours / published Reel (useful but secondary to creative quality).

### Performance reading map

| Observed pattern | Potential cause (**hypothesis**, not diagnosis) | Next test |
|---|---|---|
| Weak first-seconds retention | Hook lacks visible event, context or legible composition | Rewrite first sentence; improve opening still; preserve story |
| Strong opening, large mid-video drop | Repetitive reveals, confusing geography, predictable development | Add a real state change or remove dead beat |
| Long average watch time, few shares | Satisfying but little interpersonal share motivation | Test stronger earned emotional or ironic moment |
| Big reach, few follows | Page promise unclear / viral subject disconnected from brand | Stronger repeated visual identity; improve page bio/pinned story |
| High follower conversion, lower reach | Deep-fit audience niche, weaker discovery opening | Keep story quality; test broader but honest framing |
| Many comments about confusion | Missing clue or continuity mismatch | Fix storyboard readability rather than shortening blindly |
| A breakout Reel's next stories underperform | Audience-interest mismatch or normal volatility | Evaluate 10+ Reels rather than cloning the breakout |

**Critical:** Compare like with like (duration, genre, publication period, reporting window). Avoid overinterpreting 24-hour stats; evaluate a consistent first-48-hour snapshot and a 7-day snapshot where possible.

### Practical experiment design

1. Write **one** hypothesis, e.g. “Concrete impossible-object hooks will reduce early drop-off relative to vague atmosphere openings.”
2. Select the outcome metric *before* upload, e.g. retention at five seconds if provided.
3. Test across multiple eligible stories, not one winner vs one loser. Ideally compare at least 4–6 examples per treatment over time **when production allows**. This is a small practical sample, not rigorous causal inference.
4. Keep genre, approximate length, narrator and quality standard as comparable as reasonably possible; acknowledge remaining confounding factors.
5. Note notable differences (posting slot, seasonal news, major share event, copyrighted audio restriction).
6. Check whether retention improved **without damaging completion, shares or follower conversion**.
7. Make changes only after enough comparable evidence; preserve creative variety.

### Weekly report questions

- Which openings caused the least early abandonment where data exists?
- Which Reels generated the most **followers per non-follower reach**, not just views?
- Which emotions and genres earned **shares**?
- Where did viewers disengage, and what story beat occupied that time?
- Which story drew comments about the **plot or characters** rather than only asking for part two?
- Which production problems (anatomy, prop inconsistency, empty holds, robotic narration) recurred?
- What **one hypothesis** will we test next week?

## 11. Original idea evaluation rubric — 100 points, creative score only

This is an **editorial decision tool**, not a probability, rank prediction or Meta metric.

| Criterion | Maximum | What earns a high score |
|---|---:|---|
| 0–5-second hook | 15 | Event is visually legible and specific |
| Immediate clarity | 12 | Viewer knows protagonist/object and stakes |
| Character investment | 12 | Someone wants something and choices matter |
| Meaningful progression | 14 | Several causal developments / micro-payoffs |
| Ending payoff | 12 | Fits genre and fulfills/reframes promise |
| Organic share potential | 10 | Clear earned reason a friend would enjoy it |
| ODD FRAME recognition | 8 | Brand signature without repetitive plot |
| Still-image feasibility | 9 | Simple cast, clear staging and props |
| Originality | 8 | Distinct premise, not a disguised copy |
| **Total** | **100** | **Creative review score only** |

**Hard-fail checks override the score:** recycled third-party content; fake “true story”; AI-generated 3D/photorealism; unclear protagonist or stakes; inconsistent character/props; dependence on generated video; obligatory moral; ending that fails to honor story promise; unsafe/licensing issue.

**Interpretation:** 85+ = strong editorial candidate; 70–84 = revise targeted weaknesses; below 70 = rework concept. **These are internal cutoffs, not evidence-based success thresholds.**

## 12. Ready-to-run AI workflows

### Workflow A — Generate 10 original ideas

**Input:** genre preferences if supplied, 10 recent concepts if available, audience data if available, production constraints.

**Output:** 10 *meaningfully different* short-story concepts. For each, provide:
1. Title; genre; dominant emotion; main animal/stickman; 1-sentence premise.
2. Hook narration and first-image visual; specific curiosity question.
3. Protagonist goal/stakes; at least two middle developments.
4. Ending type with an earned payoff summary (full spoiler allowed for internal planning).
5. Why it could be shareable; why someone might follow after viewing.
6. Gold focal object/region with non-glowing color logic.
7. Still-image feasibility, risk, expected image count, draft duration.
8. 100-point **editorial** rubric score with reasons and transparent uncertainty.

Then rank top 3 based on fit and originality **without** claiming predicted reach.

### Workflow B — Build one completed Reel

**Input:** chosen concept or user-provided premise, target duration (optional), current story Bible, prior audience observations.

**Output in order:**
1. Story promise (one sentence) and specific unresolved hook question.
2. Character goals, conflict, obstacles, choices, ending type.
3. Complete original narration-only script in natural American English. Mark opening five seconds; timing provisional until actual voiceover exists.
4. Story-specific character bible (silhouette, colors, expressions, outfit, props, species/body rules).
5. Scene-by-scene still storyboard with `S001` etc.: voiceover line, scene event, framing, emotional/causal job, focus, gold placement, continuity, estimated hold, optional editorial camera movement.
6. Standalone Google Flow prompts with **strict 2D still** descriptors; **never** video-generation prompts. Use file-naming `S001.png` etc. or user's actual format.
7. Remotion plan: cuts, optional restrained movement, voice sync, narration pauses, licensed music/SFX and ending silence where meaningful.
8. Publish package: compact title, honest one- or two-sentence caption, relevant optional keywords, restrained optional CTA, optional first-comment idea if organically relevant.
9. QC checklist and recording plan for the selected hypothesis/metric.

### Workflow C — Audit a script *before* asset generation

Return:
