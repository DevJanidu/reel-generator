## 00. Read this first — non-negotiable summary

**ODD FRAME is an original, cinematic, strictly 2D, still-image storytelling entertainment brand.** We publish short, emotionally engaging fictional stories on Facebook Reels. Our stories use illustrated animals, minimalist expressive stickmen, and occasional fictional creatures. Genres may include mystery, heartbreak, betrayal, revenge, suspense, horror, adventure, fantasy, romance, loyalty, greed, compassion, dark irony, cleverness, and—occasionally—comedy or inspiration.

**We generate still images only. ## 04. Storytelling doctrine

### 4.1 Five mandatory story qualities

1. **Immediate hook:** an unusual action, dramatic contrast, emotional rupture, danger, discovery, promise, or visual question in the **first 0–5 seconds**.
2. **Clear central situation:** viewers understand who/what matters and what is at stake.
3. **Reason to continue:** a specific unanswered question or emotional outcome they want resolved.
4. **Meaningful progression:** each section changes the situation—new information, obstacle, decision, revelation, cost, action, consequence.
5. **Genre-appropriate ending:** the outcome honors the original promise or meaningfully reframes it.

**This is a quality checklist, not a fixed five-beat template.** Vary order, point of view, tone, pacing, chronology, and narrative structures where a story benefits.

### 4.2 First five seconds: the priority zone

**By 5 seconds, the viewer should understand:**
- What strange/important thing just happened?
- Whose situation are we watching, or what object matters?
- What question or consequence makes us want to continue?

Recommended opener design:
- **Opening still:** immediately legible and visually compelling, not a generic logo/title card.
- **First line:** concrete, concise, vivid, and naturally spoken; preferably introduces an event rather than background exposition.
- **Curiosity gap:** one specific unanswered question. Withhold the answer, **not all meaningful context**.
- **Sound cue:** a purposeful sound or musical contrast reinforcing the situation; avoid obnoxious whooshes or random loud jumps.
- **Pacing:** if needed, cut to a second still within the first five seconds; don't animate characters merely to simulate activity.

Avoid opening with “Once upon a time,” “In today's video,” page introductions, a long atmospheric establishing shot with no story information, or generic questions that promise nothing.

### 4.3 Retention must continue after the hook

- **Early clarity:** reveal enough context to build investment quickly.
- **Progressive disclosure:** answer one small question while opening a deeper question.
- **Escalation or complication:** changes that genuinely matter, not endless delays or repetitive narration.
- **Micro-payoffs:** intriguing details, emotional reactions, or tangible consequences throughout, not only the final second.
- **Visual variety:** alternate compositions based on story function (setting, action, close-up, detail, reaction, reveal).
- **Audio rhythm:** pauses, changes in intensity, and strategically restrained music.
- **Earned ending:** deliver the answer, emotion, or meaningful uncertainty that was promised.

**Do not artificially stretch** a 25-second premise to 60 seconds. A compelling 35-second story is better than a repetitive 60-second one. Similarly, don't rush a story that requires a longer emotional hold.

### 4.4 Endings: genre decides, not a slogan

Allowed: unsettling twist, poetic justice, solved mystery, unanswered question, tragic consequence, warm reunion, bittersweet goodbye, surprise, comedic punchline, earned victory, loss, quiet moment, or intentionally ambiguous reveal.

Not allowed as a default: “and that is why you should never trust anyone,” “never give up,” “remember to value yourself,” or other copy-pasted lessons. Do not invent a happy ending for every sad story. Do not force a twist into a story whose strongest close is quiet.

### 4.5 Genre truths and factual boundaries

Most ODD FRAME stories are fictional entertainment. If presented as **true**, **based on a real event**, **scientific**, **historical**, or **psychological fact**, separately verify and document the claim. Clearly distinguish invented fiction, adaptation, dramatization, and substantiated fact. Never invent sources or confident psychological explanations.

## 05. Recommended Reel length and pacing tests

We have not measured the ideal length for our own audience yet. Use the following only as **starting production heuristics**, not platform rules or virality guarantees:

- **Initial test range:** roughly **35–75 seconds**, with **45–60 seconds** a useful default for a compact original story.
- **Illustrative image count:** roughly **12–20 stills** for a 45–60-second Reel, but use more/fewer as needed.
- **Image hold:** often **2–4 seconds** for active story beats; some fast cuts or deliberate longer holds may be better. Change the picture **when the story needs it**.
- **Opening:** an arresting visual and sentence at 0 seconds; establish a real curiosity gap by 5 seconds.
- **Ending:** allow the emotional or narrative payoff to land; do not immediately cover it with an irrelevant outro.
- **Captions:** optional editorial text/captions where beneficial or requested; **do not bake text into Google Flow stills** unless specifically requested. Respect Facebook UI safe zones.

### 5.1 Example pacing map for a 60-second Reel (not mandatory)

| Time | Purpose | Still-image editing emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | Hook + unanswered question | Bold opening still, decisive cut/angle, clear narration |
| 5–15 s | Ground the situation | Character, location, relevant relationship or clue |
| 15–35 s | Development + small discoveries | New information, consequences, varied shot sizes |
| 35–50 s | Escalation, choice, or deeper revelation | Emotionally precise stills; faster pacing if warranted |
| 50–60 s | Payoff, reversal, or earned ambiguity | Give ending space; no forced life lesson |

A real script may use a different shape. Never sacrifice narrative logic to fit the example table.

## 06. Character system

### 6.1 Character categories

**Expressive animals:** foxes, lions, zebras, rabbits, dogs, cats, birds, wolves, snakes, bears, turtles, and other relevant species. Their behavior may be fictionalized or anthropomorphized, but the silhouette and identity must stay recognizable.

**Simple 2D stickmen:** rounded, readable faces; thin but consistent limbs; expressive eyes, brows, mouth, pose and wardrobe where useful. Protagonists may be compassionate, conflicted, flawed or brave; antagonists may be sly, manipulative or ominous. They are story characters, **not stock moral labels**.

**Occasional fictional creatures:** permitted when story and art direction call for them. Maintain the same 2D graphic language.

### 6.2 Character consistency rules

- Make a **story-specific character bible** before generating scenes: species/type, silhouette, face, proportions, coat/clothing colors, distinctive markers, emotional range, props, and permitted outfits.
- Repeat the exact approved character descriptors in each relevant still prompt; use references consistently.
- A stickman has **two arms and two legs**; avoid extra limbs or anatomically impossible hands. Animals keep species-appropriate anatomy unless deliberately stylized in the approved design.
- Keep age, size relationships, scars, props, weather damage, and clothing consistent.
- Avoid unnecessary realistic human facial anatomy; do not mix 2D styles.
- Don't require the hero, fox, lion, or villain from the **Facebook cover art** to appear in every Reel. Covers represent genre range, not a fixed recurring cast.
- Design scenes around actions still-image models can depict reliably; avoid crowded fights, overlapping bodies, complex hand-to-hand actions, and confusing object exchanges.

## 07. Visual system — MIDNIGHT GOLD

This section adapts the user's uploaded **OddFrame Design.md** (originally titled as a Say It Better still-design guide) to the current ODD FRAME name and latest still-only production rules. The original art principles are retained.

### 7.1 Mandatory rendering language

- Strictly **hand-drawn cinematic 2D illustration**.
- Confident dark ink contours, readable silhouettes, expressive simplified faces.
- Flat local colors and **limited stepped shading**, with restrained hatching/brush or paper grain.
- Layered foreground/midground/background to create cinematic depth **without photographic realism**.
- One coherent illustrated treatment for animals, stickmen, backgrounds, objects and lighting.
- No 3D, CGI, photorealism, realistic human faces, generic glossy AI style, heavy bloom, or mixed media.
- Cinematic means intentional **staging, composition, contrast and emotionally meaningful light**—not lifelike 3D rendering.

### 7.2 Locked color roles

| Color | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Midnight charcoal | `#14171C` | Deep backgrounds, shadow masses, foreground framing |
| Muted slate gray | `#41464E` | Ground, walls, foliage, secondary scenery and clothing |
| Lifted gray | `#7D838B` | Midtone readability, secondary objects and shaded details |
| Golden yellow | `#FFD43B` | **One dominant meaningful focal clue, interaction or important area** |
| Warm ivory | `#F1EBDD` | Readable faces, small light surfaces, selective typography |
| Pale yellow | `#FFF2B0` | Tiny highlights or small physically justified light cores |
| Muted ochre | `#A98428` | Golden materials in shadow and restrained reflected warmth |

General budget (flexible, not an image-analysis formula): **70–85% subdued environment; 10–25% readable characters and supporting forms; 3–10% strong yellow emphasis**.

### 7.3 The one-attention-zone rule

Before writing an image prompt, complete:

> **The viewer must notice __________ first because __________ matters to the story.**

Select the strongest narrative target:
1. A meaningful object or clue (key, letter, missing item, footprint).
2. A pivotal action or relationship (distance, exchange, empty place).
3. An important destination or boundary (door, path, opening).
4. The character's face or gesture **only when that is the critical information**.

Use the golden-yellow accent deliberately; **do not color every scarf, eye, leaf, border or piece of furniture yellow**. Preserve muted species-appropriate fur and wardrobe identity colors.

### 7.4 Gold is not automatic glow

- **Matte yellow clue:** yellow surface, shaded naturally, **no light spill**.
- **Real light source:** lantern, window, fire, or fantasy source may cast restrained yellow light on nearby surfaces.
- Do not create halos around every yellow object or yellow outlines around every figure.
- Avoid an overall mustard/yellow photo filter.
- One focal gold region should beat all other saturated accents.
- Background value separation matters as much as saturation; a clue must still read in grayscale.

### 7.5 Composition for Facebook Reels

- Canvas: **vertical 9:16, target 1080 × 1920**.
- Keep faces, hands/gestures and clues legible at mobile size.
- As conservative design guides for 1080 × 1920, keep essential information mainly inside **x=90–890, y=200–1490**, especially away from the right UI overlay and lower captions/controls. **Verify against the actual Facebook interface.**
- Vary wide, medium, close, detail, and reaction views according to story necessity.
- Use gaze direction, gestures, environmental lines and negative space to guide attention.
- The background can be detailed but must not fight the story clue.
- Do not produce baked-in subtitles, dialogue bubbles, words, logos, or watermarks unless that specific still calls for them.

### 7.6 How mood varies while keeping the brand

- Mystery: cool slate, hidden information, selective clue.
- Sadness: quiet negative space, restrained contrasts, intimate expressions.
- Horror: deeper yet still readable shadows, unclear threat, intentional off-frame space.
- Romance: gentle charcoal/ivory balance, readable connection; gold on meaningful shared object.
- Adventure: larger environmental shapes and destinations.
- Comedy: more spacious framing and lifted grays, not the same gloomy forest shot.
- Betrayal: distinct opposing poses, clear exchange, gold on the evidence or consequence.

**Yellow communicates “look here,” not automatically evil, love, magic, or importance of one particular character.**

### 7.7 Thumbnail review

Inspect stills at small mobile scale. Ask: What is happening? Whose emotion matters? What should I notice first? If unclear, fix composition, zoom, staging, or contrast **before** adding glow or extra details.

## 08. Still-image-only production system

### 8.1 Absolute restriction

**No Google Flow animations. No image-to-video clips. No text-to-video clips. No 4-second animation allocation. No generated moving character shots.**

Permitted:
- **Google Flow:** still images only.
- **Remotion / existing editor / FFmpeg:** assembling stills, direct cuts, transitions, framing adjustments, optional restrained Ken Burns movement, subtle parallax from existing layers *if available*, typographic overlays, audio sync and export.
- **Audio:** single natural narrator voiceover (ElevenLabs where used), music and sound effects with proper rights.

Editor-based motion must not be mistaken for generated character animation. Avoid excessive full-frame zoom/pan, especially repetitive moves on every still. Some stills should remain stationary to let an expression or reveal land.

### 8.2 Content production stages

1. **Select genre and concept.** Choose an emotionally distinct, original situation.
2. **State emotional promise.** Specify what the viewer is meant to wonder and feel.
3. **Write story arc.** Hook, stakes, meaningful changes, payoff; no forced moral.
4. **Draft voiceover.** Single narrator with varied delivery; natural spoken English.
5. **Build story-specific character bible.** Lock animal/stickman appearance, proportions, costume and key objects.
6. **Build beat-by-beat storyboard.** Each still performs one identifiable narrative job.
7. **Generate still-image prompts.** Fully self-contained, strict 2D, exact focal point and gold placement.
8. **Generate and approve images.** Reject 3D, inconsistent faces, anatomy errors, wrong props, misleading lighting or needless glow.
9. **Assemble in Remotion.** Sync actual images to voiceover, cut for retention, use optional limited editorial motion.
10. **Add sound design.** Purposeful ambiance, music and SFX; never bury dialogue.
11. **Quality control.** Watch in mobile-like crop; test opening 5s, continuity, readability, flow and earned ending.
12. **Export and publish.** Vertical MP4; log performance and refine future creative choices.

### 8.3 Each still needs a reason to exist

Each still should contain **at least one** of: action, reaction, important relationship, new clue, obstacle, choice, change, consequence, reveal, or emotional shift. Reject duplicates that show the same state with no additional meaning. A close-up should reveal or intensify something, not merely exist because the previous shot was wide.

### 8.4 Storyboard continuity

Track spatial left/right relationships, inventory/prop counts, objects opening or breaking, clothing changes, injuries, time of day, weather and direction of light. An important golden clue retains its material color across scenes; its **prominence** can change naturally with lighting/framing.

## 09. Sound, narration and editing

### 9.1 Narration

- One emotionally capable narrator; avoid multiple generated character voices unless deliberately authorized.
- Use natural American English; write for spoken rhythm, not essay reading.
- Vary pace, emphasis, silence and intensity according to the scene.
- Narrator should show rather than over-explain. Let pictures communicate things the audience can see.
- Avoid repetitive robotic introductions, filler, and overused phrases such as “little did he know” every Reel.
- **Do not invent exact timecodes** before voiceover is recorded: provisional timings are estimates; final timing follows actual narration length.

### 9.2 Music and effects

- Music supports genre; it must not always sound sad, frightening or motivational.
- Use ambience (forest, rain, room tone, distant voices) only where scene-appropriate.
- Use a small number of meaningful SFX for revelations, movement, an interaction, a dramatic interruption, a pause, or a final consequence.
- Make audio transitions feel intentional, not constant whooshes and booms.
- Use original or properly licensed audio; preserve proof of usage rights.

### 9.3 Edit retention without fake activity

- Start with the hook image, not a long animated logo or intro.
- Maintain visual change with a story reason; mix close-up evidence and character reactions.
- Employ small, controlled edit-based reframing **when it helps**; avoid routine zoom/pan on every image.
- Do not overuse flashy transitions; well-timed hard cuts can be more powerful.
- Protect story comprehension. Suspense without clarity becomes confusion.
- Optional captions and on-screen text should be proofread and set clear of UI overlays; no auto-generated visual text baked into illustrations by default.

### 9.4 Export baseline

- Aspect ratio: **9:16**; intended output **1080 × 1920**.
- Video: MP4/H.264, commonly **30 fps** for editor/export compatibility.
- Audio: AAC with intelligible narration.
- Test platform current technical and length limits before exporting; do not assert unverified fixed limits.

## 10. Originality, accuracy, safety and trust

- Write **original** scripts, settings, scenes and dialogue.
- Do not copy Psychlume or another creator's scripts, characters, distinctive illustrations, exact visual layouts, music or footage.
- Historical fables/public-domain tales may inspire new material, but do not merely repackage another creator's version. Transform stories substantially and credit sources where appropriate.
- Check music, image and voice generation commercial-use terms before use; policies and licenses change.
- Keep fiction clearly fictional. Verify assertions represented as true.
- A 2D cartoon is not necessarily child-directed. Handle mature psychological themes responsibly; avoid gratuitous gore and exploitative shock tactics.
- Avoid harmful stereotypes targeting ethnic, national, or religious groups.
- Do not manipulate viewers with wholly misleading open loops, fabricated “true story” claims, or engagement bait.
- Meta's monetization eligibility, country availability, thresholds and payouts must be checked in the Page's own dashboard. **Do not promise monetization in 120 days.**

## 12. Recommended master-prompt outputs and file handoff

This is a **reference document for building a separate master prompt**. It does not force the master prompt to produce everything in one answer; staged workflows may improve quality.

A well-designed ODD FRAME master prompt should support:

### Stage A — Ideas / concept selection

- 5–10 original and diverse concepts by default (or work from the user's supplied idea).
- Ten requested fields from §11.2 plus still-image feasibility.
- Explicit hook, curiosity question, intended emotion and ending type.
- User selection or user-supplied concept before production when the workflow calls for it.

### Stage B — Final story + narration

- A complete beginning-to-end original story, not a disconnected moral parable.
- First 5-second hook, character stakes, escalating developments, genre-fitting ending.
- Narration-only voiceover script, natural American English, suitable for a single narrator.
- Distinguish provisional duration from actual voiceover length.

### Stage C — Characters + continuity

- Story-specific character bible, wardrobe, body language, expression chart, props and counts.
- Locked overall 2D design language and the Midnight Gold rules.

### Stage D — Still-image storyboard

- Each scene ID `S001`, `S002`, etc.
- Its specific story beat and visible action/reaction/clue.
- Approximate duration, shot size, framing, continuity notes.
- Focal point and **exact yellow-placement mechanism**.
- Whether editorial camera movement is useful; *never* a generated animation request.

### Stage E — Google Flow still prompts

- One complete self-contained **image-generation** prompt per approved still.
- Includes composition, characters, poses, scene, prop state, 2D graphic treatment, colors, lighting logic and negatives.
- **No `image_to_video` or `text_to_video` jobs.**
- Image filenames, e.g. `S001.png` (actual extension can vary).

### Stage F — Remotion edit + audio plan

- Scene-to-voiceover timeline **measured from real audio when available**.
- Cut/hold and editorial still movement instructions.
- Music mood, SFX cue list, silence moments and narration transitions.
- Captions/typography plan only as configured for that Reel.
- Export settings, QC and Facebook publishing copy.

### Stage G — Hand-off package

For a portable, automation-friendly package, the master prompt can output Markdown + JSON source files and a ZIP when file generation is available:
