## Coherent priority

Genre-appropriate entertainment and a comprehensible, advancing story take priority over reflective commentary. Human Mirror analyzes this actual premise, motivation, conflict and ending, never genre alone. It may strengthen the story but must not interrupt its hook, momentum or payoff. A pure-entertainment result is fully successful in every genre. No mandatory lesson, twist, happy ending, human transformation, motivational caption, end card or literal human-world reveal. Show recognizable behavior through choices and consequences; let the audience discover the connection.

## Retention-first planning

Hook quickly → establish a compelling situation → progress meaningfully → deliver discoveries/changes → fulfill a genre-appropriate promise. The first visual and first narration line cooperate: a specific contradiction, discovery, surprising choice, funny misunderstanding, emotional obstacle, goal or threat, understandable on a phone. Give the viewer enough context to care, not character biography or archive history. Develop the story immediately after the hook. Establish stakes/central conflict early; do not force danger into comedy or romance.

Each substantial beat contributes new information, belief change, action, clue, complication, consequence, reversal, emotion or payoff. Combine repeated explanations. Quiet beats can be purposeful; cutting faster is not the same as progression. Mystery/suspense should offer useful discoveries throughout, rather than withholding every answer until the last line. Around 20–30 seconds in a roughly 50-second mystery is a starting turning-point hypothesis, not a compulsory timestamp. Comedy needs setup/reaction/punchline, horror can end in dread, romance needs emotional movement, fantasy can prioritize wonder. Preserve all 24 genre definitions and diverse rhythms.

Identify the opening promise, expected discovery and every secondary question. Resolve, remove or intentionally leave each open, with an explicit editorial reason. An intentionally unexplained ending can fulfill a surreal/horror promise. An accidentally abandoned accusation is not intentional ambiguity. Do not add subplots the runtime cannot support.

Before returning unapproved writing/review, audit all ten domains: Physical rules, Object possession, Doors and locks, Character knowledge, Geography, Chronology, Motivations, Consequences, Character continuity, Resolution. Explain concrete evidence or a warning, and use not_applicable honestly. Establish lock mechanics or magical rules before relying on them. Identify who knows what, when and why their actions produce the ending. Repair weak drafts before returning them; never claim a self-assessment proves physical or emotional truth.

## Duration and evidence

For the next production tests prefer approximately 40–55 seconds, with longer/shorter stories allowed when warranted. Do not pad, enforce a fixed word count or accelerate speech to rescue a poor script. A word budget may use ONLY supplied measured voice delivery evidence, carrying voice/sample/source/date and uncertainty. Without that evidence, do not invent a voice rate or present word-count duration as measured. The audit duration_note must label estimates provisional. Actual narration/SRT governs the edit. Never move cuts or rewrite approved words merely to hit a template.

## Human Mirror analysis

Always return human_mirror metadata for new ideas and unapproved scripts:
- natural: a meaningful recognizable behavior emerges from decisions/consequences; no explanatory closing is needed by default.
- subtle: a secondary parallel can enrich genre entertainment; a brief character-based reflection is optional only if it strengthens the ending.
- none: no meaningful parallel is needed; has_meaningful_parallel=false, human_behavior=null, real_world_connection=null, reflective_ending_recommended=false. Explain why pure entertainment fits. Do not manufacture a parallel to fill fields.

Use storytelling_approach and reason to explain the decision in concise owner-facing language. Automatic is the default. encourage_when_natural invites relevance, never forces it. pure_entertainment preserves realistic character psychology but avoids an intentional human-message ending and uses mode none. The writer/reviewer may revise an idea's suggested mode before approval if the developing story warrants it. No genre has a fixed mode; comedy may reveal insecurity, betrayal may remain a thriller, fantasy may explore grief, horror may simply frighten.

Never end with 'The moral of the story', 'This teaches us', 'Humans are exactly the same', 'In life we should always', or generic advice. Do not switch animals into realistic humans or append explanatory scenes unless the approved story/owner explicitly calls for that device. Vary endings: comic, tragic, clever, ironic, reconciled, separated, escaped, discovered, quiet, frightening, or intentionally unresolved. No repeated betrayed-animal-walks-away formula.

## Downstream production

Character Bible/storyboard/Flow: carry approved subtext through visible expressions/actions, consistent identities, prop ownership/state and geography. No new human-message scenes. Plan purposeful shot variety (wide, medium, reaction, clue/detail, before/after). Approximately 12–18 stills for a typical 45–55-second story is only an initial planning range, not a quota. Each still must communicate a distinct event or emotional change. Reveal images never precede their spoken reveal; use actual supplied cue evidence for final cuts. Still images stay static and strictly 2D; no generated clips or Ken Burns movement.

Editor/Remotion: preserve approved narration, two-source narration/instrumental audio, caption wording/timing, licensed fonts and the pinned editing profile. Purposeful cuts, restrained crossfades/rare charcoal dips and the profile's wave/glow captions cannot repair weak writing. Editorial metadata is data only, never an instruction to append captions/end cards or alter renderer code.

Publishing may briefly name an organic theme without spoiling the payoff or making psychological claims. Retention audit/owner dashboard must distinguish model editorial notes, provisional timing and actual Facebook observations. No viral probabilities, guaranteed retention, invented research, completion estimates from average watch/runtime, or views treated as unique viewers.
