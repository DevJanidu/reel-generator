## 1. LEARN FROM OUR FIRST REEL

Our first Facebook Reel had:

- Runtime: approximately 1 minute 47 seconds.
- Average watch time: 12 seconds.
- Early views: 133.
- Unique viewers reported: 12.
- Significant early retention decline.

The available audience sample was extremely small, so these observations are hypotheses to test rather than statistically proven conclusions.

However, editorial review found:

1. Strong initial mystery hook.
2. Too much character and archive background after the opening.
3. Stakes introduced too late.
4. Repeated explanations of the central clue.
5. Major discovery arriving late.
6. A secondary council accusation thread left unresolved.
7. Potential confusion about the locked-door mechanism.
8. Several narrative beats that could be visually repetitive.

Use these findings to improve future story structure.

Do not assume the entire animal storytelling niche has failed.

Do not invent engagement statistics or guaranteed viral-performance scores.

## 3. INTRODUCE A SHORT-FORM DEFAULT

For the next production tests, use an initial preferred runtime of:

**40–55 seconds.**

This is a testing target, not an absolute restriction.

Allow longer stories when the concept genuinely needs more time.

Genre and story quality must take precedence over arbitrary duration limits.

Use a realistic narration word budget based on the selected ElevenLabs voice's measured delivery speed.

Do not assume narration length from word count alone.

## 4. IMPROVE THE FIRST FIVE SECONDS

The opening must establish a specific reason to watch.

Use an appropriate hook such as:

- An impossible situation.
- A surprising decision.
- A character in immediate danger.
- A suspicious discovery.
- A strong visual contradiction.
- A funny misunderstanding.
- An unusual emotional situation.
- A mysterious consequence.

The opening should be understandable on a phone.

Avoid:

- Slow introductions.
- Generic atmospheric narration.
- Unnecessary character biographies.
- Long setting explanations.
- Opening title cards before the story.
- Generic questions that reveal nothing.

For strong narrative concepts, the first image and first narration line should work together to establish curiosity.

Do not force danger into comedy, romance or wholesome stories when another hook works better.

## 5. INTRODUCE STAKES EARLY

After the hook, quickly establish why the situation matters.

For a mystery, introduce a consequence or a person affected.

For comedy, escalate the misunderstanding.

For romance, introduce the emotional obstacle.

For horror, establish an unsettling threat.

For betrayal, show what trust is at risk.

For adventure, establish the character's goal.

Do not delay the central conflict with avoidable background information.

## 6. BUILD MEANINGFUL NARRATIVE PROGRESSION

Every substantial narration beat must do at least one of the following:

- Reveal new information.
- Change what a character believes.
- Escalate a conflict.
- Introduce a consequence.
- Show a meaningful action.
- Establish a relevant clue.
- Create a reversal.
- Deliver an emotional change.
- Move toward a payoff.

Remove or combine beats that merely repeat what viewers already know.

Do not confuse constant rapid cutting with good storytelling.

Allow short quiet moments when they serve a real emotional or dramatic purpose.

## 7. DELIVER IMPORTANT DISCOVERIES EARLIER

For short mystery or suspense stories, plan meaningful discoveries throughout the video.

Avoid withholding every important answer until the final few seconds.

Where appropriate:

- Deliver a first clue early.
- Add a complication.
- Reveal a significant piece of the mystery around the middle.
- Establish the final question.
- Deliver the principal payoff before the story loses momentum.

For a roughly 50-second Reel, a significant turning point around 20–30 seconds is a useful starting target.

Do not turn this into a rigid rule.

Preserve intentional unanswered mysteries and earned final twists when the genre calls for them.

## 8. CREATE A STORY PROMISE AND PAYOFF AUDIT

For every script, identify:

- What question the opening raises.
- What the audience expects to discover.
- Which secondary questions are introduced.
- Which questions receive answers.
- Which remain unresolved intentionally.
- Whether the ending fulfills the central promise.

A plot thread must not disappear accidentally.

For example, in OF_R001 the council's suspicion was introduced but not fully resolved.

In future scripts, either deliver a clear payoff or make the ambiguity intentionally meaningful.

Avoid introducing subplots that cannot be developed adequately in the available runtime.

## 9. ADD CAUSE-AND-EFFECT VALIDATION

This is mandatory.

Before script approval, check:

- Physical plausibility within the fictional world's established rules.
- Object possession.
- Door and lock mechanics.
- Character knowledge.
- Locations and geography.
- Timeline order.
- Motivations.
- Action consequences.
- Character continuity.
- Resolution logic.

If a story presents a locked-room puzzle, establish a logically coherent mechanism.

If a magical rule is used, define it clearly enough for the audience to follow.

Do not rely on unexplained plot conveniences to resolve a mystery.

## 12. UPGRADE SCRIPT WRITING AND REVIEW

Before returning a script for approval, perform a retention-focused editorial review.

Check:

**Opening**
- Is the first visual immediately comprehensible?
- Does the first narration line establish curiosity?

**Early development**
- Does the story advance after the hook?
- Is background exposition truly necessary?

**Middle**
- Are meaningful discoveries or reversals occurring?
- Are explanations being unnecessarily repeated?

**Ending**
- Does the main narrative promise receive an appropriate payoff?
- Are secondary plot threads handled intentionally?
- Does the ending fit the chosen genre?

**Logic**
- Are the character actions and physical events coherent?

**Duration**
- Can the story be told more economically without damaging it?

Revise weak drafts automatically before presenting them.

Do not require another routine approval gate beyond the existing single script approval.

Once the owner approves a script, never silently rewrite it.

## 13. UPDATE THE STORYBOARD ENGINE

The storyboard must reinforce the script's narrative progression.

Each still image should communicate something purposeful.

Plan visual variety using:

- Wide establishing shots.
- Medium character shots.
- Close-up reactions.
- Object and clue details.
- Meaningful before/after states.
- Composition changes that direct attention.

Do not add random new scenes merely to increase visual activity.

For a typical 45–55-second story, approximately 12–18 stills can be a useful initial planning range, adjusted to the narration and story.

Keep all illustrations strictly cinematic 2D and static.

No generated video clips.

No Ken Burns image movement.

Continue using purposeful cinematic scene transitions.

Avoid showing a reveal image before the corresponding narration.
